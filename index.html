Notice of readiness

Four questions decide whether the clock starts at all.

Tender a notice that fails any one of them and it is a bad notice. Laytime does not begin, and every hour until a good notice is given belongs to the owner. Switch one off and watch what happens.

An arrived ship

At the place the charter party names — the berth on a berth charter, the usual waiting place on a port charter. Lying outside it is not arrived.

Ready in every respect

Tanks inspected and accepted, cargo system lined up, free pratique granted and customs cleared. Readiness that is only partial is not readiness.

Tendered as the contract requires

In writing, to the party the charter party names, by the means it specifies. A notice to the agent when the clause says charterer is a notice to nobody.

Inside the laycan and the permitted hours

Before the cancelling date, and within whatever hours the clause allows. Outside them the notice simply waits for the window to open.

The notice bites

Turn time runs from 00:01 and laytime commences at 06:01 — the calculation below is the one that applies.

Demurrage

The hatched blocks do not count: six hours of turn time after the notice, the inward passage from anchorage to berth, and a stoppage on the vessel's own cargo pump. Miss any one of them in the calculation and somebody pays for time that was never theirs.

Terms of engagement

Six clauses, not six values.

Commercial management is a position of trust over somebody else's money. These go into the management agreement.

  1. ONEWe act for the owner and only for the owner. We do not broker the other end of your fixture, we do not trade against your position, and we hold no interest in any supplier we recommend.
  2. TWOAddress commission and brokerage appear in full on every recap and in every voyage account. We take no share of them and accept no rebate, discount or introduction fee.
  3. THREEThe voyage account is the owner's document, not a report we issue. It opens at the estimate and stays readable throughout — including the lines that are going badly.
  4. FOURTime bars are diarised the day the voyage is fixed. A claim that lapses because nobody filed it is our failure, and we treat it as one rather than as a market condition.
  5. FIVEA problem reaches the owner before it reaches the charterer's lawyers. We would rather make an uncomfortable call on the day than present a tidy summary a month later.
  6. SIXEach vessel has one named operator and one named alternate, both reachable through the trading day. You will not be routed to a general inbox during a casualty.
